# Facts in the narration

The story (Kestrel Hill, Sam, Pip, Odile, the mirror) is fiction. These four lines state real facts.

1. **"A mirror doesn't really swap left and right. It swaps front and back."** (n09)
   A plane mirror reverses an image only along the axis perpendicular to its surface (front–back); the left–right impression comes from mentally turning ourselves round to face the reflection.
   - Purdue University physics demo 7A-03: https://www.physics.purdue.edu/demos/display_page.php?item=7A-03
   - Physics FAQ, "Why does a mirror reverse left and right but not up and down?": https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/physics/General/Mirrors/mirrors.html
   - Wikipedia, "Mirror image": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image

2. **"Girls grow fastest at about eleven or twelve. Around nine centimetres in a single year."** (n16)
   MSD Manual (Professional): girls' growth spurt peaks typically between ages 11 and 12½; girls grow about 9 cm (3½ in) in the year of maximum growth. Britannica gives an average peak of about 9.0 cm/year for girls.
   - https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/growth-and-development/physical-growth-and-sexual-maturation-of-adolescents
   - https://www.britannica.com/science/human-development/Increase-in-body-size
   The doorframe marks in the film follow this curve (about 6 cm a year to 11, a 9 cm jump at 12, then slowing).

3. **"Leonardo da Vinci filled his notebooks with writing like this. To read it, you hold it up to a mirror."** (n27)
   Victoria and Albert Museum: Leonardo's notebooks are annotated "in 16th-century Italian mirror writing, which reads in reverse and from right to left."
   - https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/leonardos-notebooks

4. **"Funiculars have worked this way since the eighteen hundreds. The motor barely lifts a thing. The weight of one car lifts the other."** (n37, with the cars "passing exactly halfway", n06, and "the heavier car goes down, and it pulls the lighter one up", s16)
   In a two-car funicular the cars are attached to opposite ends of one cable and counterbalance each other, so the engine only lifts the difference (the passengers) and overcomes friction; the cars pass at a passing loop halfway. The Abt switch, the passing loop with no moving parts, was first used on the Lugano Città–Stazione funicular in 1886; the Giessbach funicular (1879) is cited as the first two-rail funicular with a passing loop.
   - Wikipedia, "Funicular": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular
   - ASME landmark #259, Giessbach funicular: https://www.asme.org/about-asme/engineering-history/landmarks/259-funicular-giessbach
   - Wikipedia, "Carl Roman Abt": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Roman_Abt
   Simplification: real funicular cars have track brakes worked by the crew and safety systems; the film's "red lever in each car" and the two cars rolling apart under gravity alone, with no power, dramatise the counterweight principle.

Not used (considered): the "end of history illusion" (Quoidbach, Gilbert & Wilson, Science 2013) and the future-self studies (Hershfield et al.) — the earlier mirror film already used them; this film only echoes the idea in its last lines without stating them as facts.
